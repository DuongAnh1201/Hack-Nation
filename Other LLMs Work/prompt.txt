@Deep research You are a senior nanophotonics researcher tasked with designing a passive daytime radiative cooling coating.

Objective:
Design a thin-film multilayer optical coating deposited on a Silver (Ag) substrate that achieves sub-ambient cooling under direct sunlight (AM1.5 spectrum, clear sky, 8–13 µm atmospheric transmission window).

Constraints:

1. Maximum layers: At most 5 layers.
2. Search space materials: Only choose from [SiO2, Si3N4, Al2O3, TiO2, MgF2] on an Ag substrate. (HfO2 is strictly excluded due to cost and scarcity).
3. Thickness per layer: Each layer thickness must be strictly bounded between 10 nm and 1,000 nm.
4. Target performance: Achieve a net cooling power P_net ≥ 39.8 W/m² at T = T_amb = 300 K, matching or outperforming the landmark Stanford 7-layer HfO2/SiO2 benchmark (Raman et al., Nature 2014).

Deliverables:

1. The exact sequence of materials (from top surface down to the Ag substrate) and exact thickness in nanometers for each layer.
2. Calculated solar reflectance (0.3–2.5 µm) and atmospheric window emissivity (8–13 µm).
3. Physical rationale for refractive index contrast and phononic resonance modes.
4. Cited evidence and step-by-step hypothesis verification.
